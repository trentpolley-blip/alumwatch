How the reads are checked

The tables come from sources and are reconciled against each other — every game log here sums to the season average printed beside it. "The read" is different: it is written prose, so every factual claim in it was audited against independent sources on Oct. 30, 2026, and eight were corrected or cut. Claims that rested on four or five games now say so, and a figure nobody could source was removed rather than softened.

The errors that got through the first pass are worth knowing about, because they are the kinds that recur: a stale fact that used to be true, a stat belonging to a different Wildcat, and a streak counted one season too long. Anything written here should be re-checked, not carried forward.

How this list is built

A player qualifies only if Arizona was the last college he played for. That keeps the one-and-dones who went straight to the league, and cuts anyone who transferred away to finish somewhere else — Henri Veesaar, Oumar Ballo, Kylan Boswell, Alex Barcello and Emmanuel Akot all played multiple seasons in Tucson but ended their college careers elsewhere.

The form chart plots points per game this season, with the gold line at that player's own average. Bars at or above the line are green. It only appears where a real game log exists.

Career tables mix leagues deliberately — an NBA row sits next to a Serie A row next to a G League row, because that is what these careers actually look like. Where a season is missing it is marked, not filled in.

Known gaps

  • The G League season tips off around Nov. 10, so no two-way player has affiliate numbers yet.
  • Tubelis's nine EuroLeague games and Henderson's three EuroCup games exist only as averages — the official site is still serving last season's game data.
  • Japan, Australia, Poland and Mexico publish little or nothing per game.
  • Absences are derived by comparing each player's log against his team's schedule, so a missed game shows as "did not play" with no reason unless one was confirmed.
  • Minutes in the game logs are whole numbers, so a chart's minutes average can sit a tenth off the published season figure.
